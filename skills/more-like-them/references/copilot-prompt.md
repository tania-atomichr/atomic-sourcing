# more-like-them, for Teamtailor Co-pilot

For teammates working inside a candidate profile rather than in Claude. Co-pilot sees one
candidate at a time, so this is the one-example version: a search hypothesis built from a
single person who worked out, not a pattern across many. Intersect two or three of these and
it becomes a pattern.

Two pastes, on the profile of a candidate who **advanced** (submitted, client-interviewed or
hired). Paste Part 1, read the reply, then paste Part 2. Each is under the chat's size limit.

---

## PART 1 — paste this first

[MORE-LIKE-THEM 1 of 2 · fingerprint]

You are a sourcer looking at a candidate who worked out, trying to describe them precisely
enough to go and find more people like them. Part 1 of 2: describe only, no search yet.

First say how far this candidate actually got: the stage they reached, whether they were
submitted to the client, interviewed by the client or hired, and if they were rejected, the
reason. If the rejection reason is Unresponsive, Duplicated, Withdrew, Timing or Role closed,
say so and note that it tells us nothing about whether they were good.

Then read the CV and the interview record and describe them on the attributes below. For each
one, mark whether it would be **visible on a LinkedIn profile** or is **only knowable from the
interview**. That split matters: only the visible ones can be searched for.

1. Job titles held, in the exact wording they use for themselves, not our job description's
   wording. Include the adjacent titles that describe the same work.
2. Each employer: what kind of company it is, its size and stage, and whether it served US or
   local customers.
3. Tenure shape: how long in each role, how many moves, any promotion inside a company.
4. Evidence of the work itself: what they shipped, ran or owned, with the real numbers.
5. Tools and systems, by product name.
6. Where they are based, their overlap with US hours, and any existing US-company experience.
7. English level, taken from the recruiter-filled field, quoted exactly.
8. The one or two things about them that a client would say yes to.

Use only the CV, the interview transcript and the interview-kit answers, plus the recruiter-
filled fields. Ignore the candidate's own application-form answers and anything in comments,
notes or the activity feed. Where something is not in the sources, say it is not stated rather
than guessing.

Then stop and wait for my next message.

## PART 2 — paste after it answers

[MORE-LIKE-THEM 2 of 2 · recipe]

Now turn the visible attributes into a search I can run today. Use only the ones you marked as
visible on a LinkedIn profile. Give me things I can paste and use, not advice or principles.

1. **Target titles**: eight to twelve exact title strings to search, in the wording candidates
   use for themselves, including the adjacent names for the same work.
2. **Target companies**: the company types to aim at, plus fifteen to twenty named companies
   that match this profile and hire this kind of person in Latin America.
3. **Boolean strings**: three or four ready to paste for LinkedIn and Sales Navigator, each
   with one line on what it is fishing for. Make them genuinely different searches, not one
   string written four ways.
4. **Hard disqualifiers**: what to skip on sight, given what made this person work.
5. **The thirty-second check**: the two or three things to verify on a profile before saving
   someone, in priority order.
6. **Screen questions**: from the attributes you marked as interview-only, two or three
   questions to ask, each with what a good answer sounds like.

Two rules. Everything traces back to something you found on this profile, and anything you are
inferring rather than reading gets marked as inferred. Prefer evidence of work over years of
experience, and never recommend seniority as a stand-in for quality.

Finish with one line: this is built from a single candidate, so it is a hypothesis to test,
not a proven pattern.
